Episode 6: The Path to Economic Sovereignty
What would it take for Africa to reclaim its agency and build a truly reparative future? In this sixth and final episode, we explore reparations not simply as compensation for historical harms, but as a transformative process for dismantling the systems that continue to reproduce inequality today. Drawing on Pan-Africanism and the philosophy of Ubuntu, we examine what economic sovereignty requires beyond financial redress: reclaiming historical memory, strengthening political coordination, and transforming the institutions and educational systems that shape Africa's future. The discussion makes the case that reparations must begin with reclaiming African agency and building a shared vision across the continent and its diaspora. From teaching Africa's true history and grounding policy in African value systems to coordinating reparations struggles and developing institutions capable of advancing a collective agenda, the episode asks what it would take to turn reparative justice into a foundation for Africa's future.

In this episode, we cover:


Reparations as transformation: Why reparative justice must go beyond compensation to address the systems that continue to produce inequality.

Reclaiming African agency: The role of historical memory, education, Pan-Africanism, and Ubuntu in shaping a shared vision for Africa's future.

Building a reparative future: The importance of political coordination and strong African institutions in advancing economic sovereignty and a collective reparations agenda.

Featured guest


Dr. Gnaka Lagoke - Associate Professor of History and Pan-Africana Studies  and Chair of the Pan-Africana Studies Department, Lincoln University, PA, Founder of the Ubuntu Philosophy Foundation

Moderated by:


Catherine Mithia - Policy Analyst and Advocacy Officer- Sovereign Debt Management at AFRODAD

Lavender Namdiero - Projects Coordinator at African Futures Lab. 


This podcast is a joint collaboration between the African Forum and Network on Debt and Development (AFRODAD) and African Futures Lab (Afalab) .
