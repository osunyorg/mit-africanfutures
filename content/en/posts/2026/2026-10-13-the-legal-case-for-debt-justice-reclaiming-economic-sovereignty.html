Episode 4: The Legal Case for Debt Justice: Reclaiming Economic Sovereignty
Can international law be mobilized to challenge and overturn Africa's illegitimate debt burdens? In this second episode, we shift the focus from historical critique to immediate legal accountability. We unpack the international legal landscape to examine how legal definitions of odious and illegitimate debt can serve as strategic mechanisms for African nations to challenge extractive financial obligations. We also spotlight the growing diplomatic momentum across the continent-from Ghana's leadership at the United Nations to reparative justice efforts in Namibia and the Democratic Republic of Congo addressing legacies of colonial genocide and resource exploitation. Join us as we explore how debt justice and legal reparations form an inseparable, unified struggle to reclaim African economic sovereignty and agency.

In this episode, we cover:


Legal frameworks for debt: Deconstructing odious and illegitimate debt under international law.

Leveraging international law: Concrete mechanisms for African states to dispute extractive financial burdens.

Continental momentum: How nations like Ghana, Namibia, and the DRC are advancing reparative justice frameworks.

Featured guests

Dr. Henry Paul Gichana - Devolution and research lead at Katiba Institute, specializing in constitutional compliance, public interest litigation, and governance.
Chioneso Samantha Kanoyangwa is a legal and policy researcher working at the intersection of economic justice, sovereign debt, and human rights in Africa.

Moderated by:

Afshin Nazir -  Policy Research & Advocacy Officer - Legal Analysis & Advocacy at AFRODAD Lavender Namdiero - Program Coodinator at AfaLab


This podcast is a joint collaboration between the African Forum and Network on Debt and Development (AFRODAD) and African Futures Lab (Afalab) .
